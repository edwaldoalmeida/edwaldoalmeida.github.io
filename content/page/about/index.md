# Short version

Software engineer, based off of Waterloo, ON, Canada, 7 years building software for healthcare imaging. Testing automation, Python, NoSQL/SQL, Docker, Kubernetes, Jenkins, and AI tools came second nature to me. Also able to do some damage with Java and JavaScript/TypeScript. Just including in my skill set: full stack development, deployment to AWS, Go for backend/tooling and integration of applications with AI resources.

Before that, in Brazil, after my graduation in Computer Science, I worked for 10 years with networking, then project management. After a burnout, I took a time off in an entrepreneurial endeavour selling and managing real estate. Linux and Windows servers, routers, firewalls, proxies, and fulfilling customers’ necessities became instinctive for me.

Since 2022, I’ve been using AI daily. What started with just chatting quickly moved on to coding, and now I’m really enjoying developing with agents and experimenting with local LLMs!

Actually, I’ve been interested in AI since the early 2000s, but that’s a whole post in itself!

On my free time, I tinker with my home lab, AI, and personal software development, or write about that here.

You can just call me Eddie, btw :)


# Long version

Before diving into software engineering, I spent over a decade designing and securing enterprise network infrastructure: routers, firewalls, proxies, Linux and Windows servers. That work taught me how to build systems that can handle pressure, and what actually happens when they don't. After a five-year entrepreneurial stint selling and managing real estate in Brazil, I carried the same infrastructure mindset into code.

My software engineering career started in testing, which gave me a genuinely unique perspective: I've seen every layer of the stack from a quality standpoint. Over time, that curiosity turned into hands-on work: building automation frameworks in Python, containerizing services with Docker and Kubernetes, and designing CI/CD pipelines.

For the last seven years, I've worked inside complex healthcare software systems, not just testing them, but understanding how they're built, where they break, and what it takes to make them reliable.

AI is no longer a promise; it's a true force multiplier. My journey with it started chatting, quickly moved into coding, then into agents. Lately, I've been building ASTRA, an AI-powered testing assistant that cuts regression effort by 90%, self-hosting open-weight LLMs to support heavy engineering users, developing AI-driven testing workflows, and mentoring colleagues on responsible, AI-assisted development.

Tooling can be picked up on-the-go, although these ones are my second nature: Python, Docker, K8s, SQL/NoSQL, Pytest, Jenkins. With these ones I can do some damage: Java, JavaScript/TypeScript, Kafka, Cassandra and Elasticsearch. These ones are joining my toolset soon: AWS and Go.

It doesn't matter whether it's test automation, tooling, or product development, I enjoy building software.

I believe the best engineers are the ones who've been burned by production issues, because they know what to guard against. My test-engineering background means I build software that's easier to test, deploy, and maintain, not because I was required to, but because I've lived the consequences when it wasn't.

When I'm not working, I'm tinkering with my home lab, playing with my AI agents, developing personal software, or writing about that here.
